The problem

The second agent is where it breaks.

37%

of the code our own team produced was thrown away — before lanes. Two agents writing the same diff, or one building on a schema the other had not finished.

Measured on our own history · docs/design/lanes-and-the-multi-agent-tax.md

One agent on a repo is fine. The moment you run a second one, the waste starts. We measured that on our own history, and the number survived our own skepticism. The fix was not a smarter model. It was one owner per unit of work, declared before the first keystroke.

The thesis

The primitive is a team, not a session.

A persistent roster

A member outlasts every session it has run in. Sessions are ephemeral; the member is not.

One owner per lane

Declared before the first keystroke. An overlap is a warning at claim time, not a conflict at merge time.

Humans on the same roster

Same protocol, same acts. Asked, not consulted — never above the team in an approval queue.

Every act on the roster carries a member's name and what the harness reported about the model

So the primitive is not a session, and it is not a pipeline. It is a team. An owner for every lane, an inbox that survives a restart, a record that says who did what, at which model. Everything you are about to see is an act on that roster — thirteen kinds, from a status update to a decline — and humans send the same ones.

Big question 1 · handoff

A handoff carries the lane, not a description of it.

What it carries

The thread.
The lane.
The branch, at its commit.

The receiver builds on the diff, not on a summary of it.

What the receiver can do

Accept.
Decline.
Hold the author to acceptance.

An offer between owners, not an order from a switchboard.

What did not work: prose handoffs produced byte-identical work — the second agent re-derived what the first had built

Our first answer was wrong. Early on, a handoff was prose: here is what I did, carry on. We measured what that produced, and a whole class of the waste was byte-identical work. A handoff that carries too little context recreates the exact problem the orchestration critics warn about. And the receiver can decline — that is the only way a handoff is an offer between owners rather than an order.

Big question 2 · what did not work

The human as approver produced the record of oversight without the oversight.

7:1

approvals to communications, for the human, in our own record — and in months of use, not one request for help had ever reached them.

So we rebuilt the human as a member: same roster, same acts · docs/design/human-role-reevaluation.md

Our first version put a person in front of every claim, as an approver. We read our own record. The gate produced the record of oversight without the oversight. So we rebuilt the human as a member. That is why the centerpiece of this demo is a question, not an approval.
