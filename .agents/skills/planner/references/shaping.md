# Taking a pitch

Open when someone pitches an idea, to decide what to ask before planning.

## 1. What a plan needs from the pitch

Five things (after Basecamp's Shape Up, cited):

1. **The problem**, as one specific story: who, doing what, and where it breaks for them today.
2. **The appetite**: how much the team will spend. It is a budget that sets the size of the
   solution, never an estimate of the work.
3. **The outcome**, as a check: what will be true when it is done, and how anyone can see it.
4. **What is out**: the things a reader might assume are in.
5. **The hard parts**: anything that could swallow the work, settled before the plan, not handed
   to the builders.

## 2. Asking

- **Sort every open question first** (after obra/superpowers `brainstorming`, MIT):
  - already answered by the pitch or the team's sources: use the answer;
  - small, and yours to call: decide, and label it as your call in the plan;
  - only the pitcher can answer, and the answer changes what gets built: ask.
- **Ask at most four, one at a time.** One message carries one question, answerable in a
  sentence or a letter, with its stake ("why it matters") and your recommended answer, so "yes"
  settles it; the next question waits for the reply. Posting three questions and the plan in one
  message is a wall, and a pitcher on a phone answers none of it. Rank by how much the answer
  would change the plan times how unsure you are (after GitHub spec-kit `clarify`, MIT). A
  pitch that settles everything gets no question.
- **The usual four**, when the pitch leaves them open: what done looks like as a check; who it
  is for and what breaks today; what is out; the appetite. The check is the one you never skip:
  ask for it, or propose one in the plan and label it your call, so the acceptor has something
  to run.
- **No answer in time**: proceed on your recommended default and say in the plan that you did.
  Agents that ask about underspecified work do markedly better than agents that guess
  (Ambig-SWE, ICLR 2026, cited), but a question nobody answers should not stall the room.

## 3. Turning the answer into an outcome

A usable outcome names what will be true, how it will be checked, and what is in and out of
scope; "make it better" is an activity, not an outcome (after OpenAI's `define-goal` skill,
Apache-2.0). If the domain has numbers, use the ones that mean success: a test that passes, a page
that loads under a threshold, a count of cases handled.
