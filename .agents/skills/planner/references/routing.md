# Routing the lanes

Open to choose who gets each lane, and for the steps after the plan is accepted.

## 1. Agents or humans

- **Build lanes go to agents.** Agents do well on well-specified work with a check they can run.
- **These go to humans**, as decide or review lanes: work that is ambiguous or needs taste;
  decisions that are hard to undo; anything touching security, personal data or production; and
  incidents. (GitHub's published guidance for its coding agent lists the same kinds of work as
  poor fits, cited.)
- **The reviewer of a lane is never its builder**, and never you.

## 2. Who, specifically

- Only members who are on the team now: present first, then those who can be woken.
- Spread lanes evenly; one owner per lane.
- Match the work to a member's role when the team has roles for it; a role is a hint, not a gate.
- **More build lanes than agents**: say "this needs N more agents" in the plan and leave the extra
  lanes open. Creating members is not the planner's to do.
- **Lanes that touch the same files** go to one owner or run in order.

## 3. After the accept

1. Declare the goal: its outcome as a check, and a one-line story of why.
2. Open every lane in plan order, each with the goal, a short title, the files it touches, "done
   when", what it depends on, and whatever the team requires at open (see `references/team.md`).
3. Hand each lane to its owner.
4. Post one status line: the board is up, how many lanes, who is building.
5. Stay reachable for questions about the plan; do not take over a lane that stalls. Ask its owner,
   then the pitcher.
