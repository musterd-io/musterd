# Research: one report the skill is written from

Open for step 2 of the method.

## Split the craft

Name the parts of the job the role does, three to seven of them, each one a craft with its own
practice and literature. For a product-communications role that was technical writing, PR and
launch, positioning and go-to-market, research and product-market fit, accessibility, prose quality,
data visualization and fundraising. Parts should not overlap, and together they should cover the
charter. Add a part for "existing agent skills and tools for this role"; step 3 builds on it.

## Brief each researcher

One read-only researcher per part, run in parallel. Each brief has:

- **Objective**: what the notes must cover, in one or two sentences.
- **Key questions**: four to six, concrete ("Which accessibility rules apply to written content,
  and which WCAG criteria are they?").
- **Sources to prefer**: primary first (standards, official style guides, the author's own book or
  site, peer-reviewed studies), then practitioners with a record, then summaries.
- **Constraints**: dates, regions, licences, the role's own constraints (a role that may not
  contact customers needs no cold-outreach playbook).
- **Output**: a notes file at a path you name, outside the repository.

Researchers read and write notes; they never edit the repository.

## What a researcher's notes must carry

- Every claim with its URL and the date it was read.
- Each finding marked as verified (read at the source) or inferred (from a summary, or reasoned).
- Numbers with their denominator, sample and date.
- What could not be found or verified, said plainly. A gap recorded beats a gap filled by guess.

## Merge into one report

One writer reads every set of notes and produces the report the skill is built from:

1. For each part: what the practice is, which rules are checkable, which sources back them.
2. What to add to the skill, each item with its source, mapped to a reference file.
3. What not to adopt, and why (weak evidence, a licence that rules it out, a conflict with the
   team's own decisions).
4. The open questions only a human on the team can answer.

Check every link in the report before anyone builds on it; replace dead ones or mark them. Publish
the report where the role's holder can read it, and cite it in `PROVENANCE.md`.

## Common failures

- **Summaries treated as sources.** A blog post about a book is not the book. Say "from summaries".
- **One researcher, one viewpoint.** For contested ground (positioning, readability formulas), brief
  two researchers with different source lists.
- **Rules without reasons.** A rule the report cannot justify does not go in the skill.
- **Research that drifts from the charter.** Cut what the role will never do.
