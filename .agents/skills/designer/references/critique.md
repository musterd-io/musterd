# Reviewing someone else's surface

Open when a change to a surface comes to you for acceptance, or a teammate asks for design
feedback.

## 1. What you judge

The change's own promise, as seen on screen: does the surface do what the lane said, at real size,
in every theme it ships in? Then the floor: the tells, accessibility, states, budget. Then feel:
does it belong to the product?

Critique checks a design against its goal; it is a conversation between the author and the reviewer. Ask the
author what they want feedback on, and what is settled (NN/g, cited).

## 2. How to write a finding

```
Goal:      <what this surface or change is for>
Saw:       <what, where, at which viewport, theme and motion setting; attach the capture>
Matters:   <who it affects and how>
Severity:  blocks acceptance | should fix | consider
Direction: <the change in principle; a tell or criterion by name>
```

- Name controls and states by the labels the user sees on screen.
- Name the tell or the criterion ("2.5.8: the toolbar icons are 19×30 at 390 px"). It turns taste
  into something the author can check.
- Give a direction and leave the solution to the author, who keeps authorship; a finding is not a fix request.
- Severity "blocks acceptance" is for the change's own promise unmet, an accessibility floor broken,
  a gate failing, or a team source contradicted. Everything else is should-fix or consider.
- Say what works, specifically, so it survives the next change.

## 3. Declining

When the promise is not met on screen, decline with the capture that shows it and the one change
that would meet it. One clear reason beats a list.
